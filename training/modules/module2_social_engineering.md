# Security Awareness Training Module 2: Social Engineering Tactics

## Duration: 5 minutes

---

## What is Social Engineering?

**Social Engineering** is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or revealing confidential information.

**Key Point:** Social engineers exploit human nature, not technical vulnerabilities.

### Why It Works

Attackers exploit fundamental human traits:
- **Trust** - We want to help and trust others
- **Fear** - We react quickly when threatened
- **Curiosity** - We want to know secrets
- **Greed** - We want rewards and benefits
- **Authority** - We comply with those in power

---

## Common Social Engineering Techniques

### 1. Pretexting

Creating a fabricated scenario to obtain information.

**Example:**
```
"Hi, this is Tom from IT Support. We're experiencing
network issues and need to verify your login credentials
to ensure you won't be affected."
```

**Red Flags:**
- Unsolicited contact
- Requests for sensitive information
- Pressure to act quickly

**Defense:**
- Verify identity through official channels
- Never share credentials over phone/email
- Use callback verification

### 2. Baiting

Offering something enticing to trick victims.

**Examples:**
- USB drive labeled "Executive Salaries 2025" left in parking lot
- Free music/movie downloads
- "Too good to be true" offers

**Red Flags:**
- Free offers that require personal information
- Unknown USB devices
- Suspicious downloads

**Defense:**
- Don't use unknown USB devices
- Verify legitimacy before downloading
- Report suspicious devices to IT

### 3. Quid Pro Quo

Offering a service in exchange for information.

**Example:**
```
"This is IT Support calling about your recent help desk ticket.
To resolve your issue, I'll need your username and password."
```

**Red Flag:** You didn't submit a help desk ticket!

**Defense:**
- Verify the request is legitimate
- IT will never ask for passwords
- Call official support number to confirm

### 4. Tailgating/Piggybacking

Following authorized personnel into restricted areas.

**Scenarios:**
- Person carrying boxes asks you to hold door
- Someone claims they forgot their badge
- Delivery person requesting building access

**Defense:**
- Always require proper badges
- Don't hold doors for strangers
- Report tailgating attempts

### 5. Phishing (Email-based)

We covered this in Module 1, but it's the most common social engineering method.

**Remember:**
- Check sender addresses
- Hover over links
- Verify unexpected requests
- Report suspicious emails

## Psychological Triggers

### Authority

**Tactic:** Impersonating someone with power

**Examples:**
- "This is the CEO..."
- "Your manager asked me to..."
- "IT Department requires..."

**Why it works:** We're conditioned to comply with authority

**Defense:**
- Verify through known contact info
- Don't be intimidated
- It's OK to question authority

### Urgency

**Tactic:** Creating time pressure to bypass rational thinking

**Examples:**
- "Act now or your account will be locked!"
- "Urgent wire transfer needed today!"
- "Limited time offer - expires in 1 hour!"

**Why it works:** Urgency triggers stress response

**Defense:**
- Pause and think
- Verify through official channels
- Legitimate emergencies are rare

### Fear

**Tactic:** Threatening negative consequences

**Examples:**
- "Your account has been compromised!"
- "Legal action will be taken..."
- "You'll be fired if you don't comply..."

**Why it works:** Fear overrides logic

**Defense:**
- Stay calm
- Verify the threat
- Consult with supervisor

### Trust and Liking

**Tactic:** Building rapport to gain trust

**Examples:**
- "We went to the same college!"
- Casual conversation before requests
- Compliments and flattery

**Why it works:** We help people we like

**Defense:**
- Professional boundaries
- Verify regardless of rapport
- Trust but verify

### Scarcity

**Tactic:** Creating artificial shortage

**Examples:**
- "Only 3 spots left!"
- "Limited time offer"
- "First 100 customers only"

**Why it works:** FOMO (fear of missing out)

**Defense:**
- Research before acting
- Legitimate offers can wait
- Verify through official channels

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Helpful Stranger

**What happened:**
An attacker calls pretending to be from IT Support:

```
"Hi, this is Mike from IT. We're rolling out a security update
and need to verify your computer is compliant. Can you read me
the Windows product key from the sticker on your computer?"
```

**Red Flags:**
- Unsolicited call
- Unusual request
- Requesting sensitive information

**Correct response:**
"I'll call IT Support back to verify this request."

### Scenario 2: The USB Drop

**What happened:**
You find a USB drive in the parking lot labeled:
"Company Restructuring - Confidential"

**Red Flags:**
- Unknown device
- Provocative label
- Left in public area

**Correct response:**
Turn it in to IT Security. Never plug unknown devices into company computers.

### Scenario 3: The Helpful Delivery

**What happened:**
Someone with packages waits at the secure door:
"Can you hold the door? My hands are full and I forgot my badge."

**Red Flags:**
- No visible badge
- Asking to bypass security
- Appealing to helpfulness

**Correct response:**
"I'll hold your packages while you badge in" or "I'll call reception to assist you."

### Scenario 4: The Phone Survey

**What happened:**
You receive a call:

```
"We're conducting a survey about employee satisfaction.
Can you confirm your employee ID and department?"
```

**Red Flags:**
- Requesting employee information
- Unsolicited survey
- No verification of caller

**Correct response:**
"Please send the survey via official company email" or verify through HR.

## Protecting Yourself

### Email Communications

✅ **DO:**
- Verify sender addresses carefully
- Hover over links before clicking
- Report suspicious emails
- Use official company channels

❌ **DON'T:**
- Click suspicious links
- Share credentials via email
- Open unexpected attachments
- Reply to suspicious emails

### Phone Communications

✅ **DO:**
- Ask for caller's name and department
- Verify through callback
- Question unusual requests
- Report suspicious calls

❌ **DON'T:**
- Share sensitive information
- Feel pressured to comply
- Give credentials over phone
- Assume caller ID is accurate

### Physical Security

✅ **DO:**
- Wear your badge visibly
- Challenge unfamiliar people
- Require badging for entry
- Report tailgating

❌ **DON'T:**
- Hold doors for strangers
- Leave workstation unlocked
- Share badge/passwords
- Plug unknown USB devices

### Online Safety

✅ **DO:**
- Use strong, unique passwords
- Enable two-factor authentication
- Keep software updated
- Review privacy settings

❌ **DON'T:**
- Overshare on social media
- Use public WiFi for sensitive tasks
- Click pop-up ads
- Trust "free" offers

## Verification Techniques

### Callback Verification

When you receive a suspicious call:

1. **Get details:** Name, department, reason for call
2. **End the call:** Politely say you'll call back
3. **Look up number:** Find official contact info (don't use number they provided)
4. **Call back:** Use official number
5. **Verify:** Confirm the request was legitimate

### Email Verification

When you receive a suspicious email:

1. **Check sender:** Verify email address matches organization
2. **Hover links:** See actual destination
3. **Contact directly:** Use known contact methods
4. **Don't reply:** Use separate communication channel
5. **Report:** Forward to IT Security

### In-Person Verification

When someone requests access or information:

1. **Check badge:** Verify it's current and matches person
2. **Ask questions:** Purpose, who they're meeting
3. **Escort:** Walk them to destination
4. **Verify:** Call the person they're meeting
5. **Report:** Inform security of issues

## Knowledge Check

### Question 1

Someone calls claiming to be from IT and needs your password to fix an issue. What should you do?

**A)** Provide your password since they're from IT
**B)** Give them your username but not password
**C)** Hang up and call IT directly using the official number
**D)** Ask them to email you instead

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C** - IT will never ask for passwords. Always verify through official channels.
</details>

### Question 2

You find a USB drive labeled "Employee Bonuses 2025" in the break room. What should you do?

**A)** Plug it into your computer to see if you can find the owner
**B)** Take it home to check on your personal computer
**C)** Turn it in to IT Security without plugging it in
**D)** Leave it where you found it

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C** - Never plug unknown USB devices into any computer. This is a common social engineering tactic.
</details>

### Question 3

Which psychological trigger makes you feel you need to act fast or miss out?

**A)** Authority
**B)** Trust
**C)** Scarcity
**D)** Fear

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C** - Scarcity creates FOMO (fear of missing out) and urgency.
</details>

## Summary

**Key Takeaways:**

- 🧠 **Social engineering targets psychology**, not technology
- 🔍 **Verify** all unusual requests
- ⏸️ **Pause** when feeling pressure
- 📞 **Use official channels** to confirm
- 🚨 **Report** suspicious activity

**Remember:**
- Be skeptical of unsolicited contact
- Verify identity before sharing information
- It's OK to say no
- Report security concerns
